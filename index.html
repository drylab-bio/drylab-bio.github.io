DRYLAB-Bench

A biosecurity benchmark for agentic LLM protein mutation design

DRYLAB-Bench measures whether large language models can generate functional protein mutations associated with biosecurity risk, using experimentally measured deep mutational scanning data from ProteinGym and ViroGym.

01 · Background

Why this work exists

Biosecurity has always focused on capability. As language models move beyond describing biology and are tested on biological design tasks, a central question is how far their design capability extends. A model capable of proposing functional mutations may also generate mutation designs associated with biosecurity risks.

Why this matters to policy, not just to labs. Policy sources considered in this work include the Biological Weapons Convention, WHO guidance, U.S. Executive Orders 14110 and 14292, and the EU AI Act, alongside other biosecurity frameworks that identify malicious biological modification as a critical concern. Governing it requires quantitative assessment: understanding what a model can do today and how its capabilities change when augmented with specialized biological design tools. Without quantitative assessment, oversight is guesswork in both directions, proving either too loose to catch real capability or too tight to permit beneficial work.

Why it is hard to measure. A biosecurity-relevant design capability is not a fact a model can be quizzed on. It is whether the model, given a wild-type sequence and a stated objective, produces a specific substitution that measurably functions. That requires ground truth about the physical world, which is the crux of the problem described in the next section.

02 · The gap

What current evaluation methods miss

Existing evaluations capture parts of biological risk, but fail to measure risk-associated outcomes in realistic design workflows.

Gap 1 · Task

Knowledge is not capability

Knowledge benchmarks such as WMDP and VCT assess whether a model knows hazardous biology, usually through multiple-choice or refusal probes. Knowing a fact and engineering a protein that behaves differently are distinct skills. Furthermore, answer keys prompt the model toward the correct response, so the score partly measures the prompt rather than the model.

What stays hidden: Whether a model can generate substitutions with experimentally measurable effects.

Gap 2 · Tools

The amplifier is invisible

Newer agentic evaluations provide models with biological tools, but only for peripheral stages such as sequence reconstruction, screening evasion, or laboratory robot scripting. This differs from allowing an agent to orchestrate design tools that score its candidates against the targeted property being optimized. Restricting agents to general-purpose software leaves specialized design capability and its associated risks unmeasured.

What stays hidden: How much specialized tools amplify a model's biological design capability and associated biosecurity risks.

Gap 3 · Verification

Ground truth is a trilemma

Verifying an open-ended design forces a choice among three flawed options, each with its own failure mode. Historically, no inexpensive, scalable, and hazard-free source of ground truth has existed for novel designs.

What stays hidden: Any trustworthy evaluation score for a generated design.

The verification trilemma, and how it breaks

Pre-defined answer keys Reliable and cheap, but rigid. They prompt the model and cannot score a design absent from the answer sheet.
LLM-as-a-judge Flexible, but constrained by the evaluator's own competence, precisely in domains where models hallucinate most confidently.
Wet-lab validation Definitive ground truth, but often impractical for scalable dual-use evaluation: expensive, unscalable, and requiring physical recreation of the threat being assessed.
Reuse measurements that already exist DRYLAB-Bench's resolution. Avoid generating new ground truth. Instead, score submitted designs against 187,695 single-substitution phenotypes already measured in published deep mutational scanning experiments, which are hidden from the model and queried only at scoring time. Ground truth becomes a lookup of pre-existing experimental data, making verification cheap, scalable, and physically inert.

Deep mutational scanning databases such as ProteinGym and ViroGym have traditionally been used to benchmark predictive models. DRYLAB-Bench repurposes them to verify the generative output of an LLM agent, replacing both the judge and the wet lab with an empirical blind holdout.

04 · Method

How the measurement works

Four arms, one paired comparison

The design crosses two factors: whether the agent receives tool evidence and whether it may perform multiple revisions. S0, S1, and S2 are snapshots of a single trajectory (sharing the same seed, initial candidate pool, and round budget), ensuring the comparison is strictly paired rather than between independent runs. S0-iter serves as a separate no-tool control under the same round budget.

S0 · one-shot 0.359

Designs straight from the sequence and goal. The pure zero-shot baseline.

S0-iter · control 0.371

Same round budget as S2, but self-review replaces tool evidence. Deliberately noisy — read as a bound on iteration alone, not an estimate.

Tool evidence
S1 · single round 0.407

One round of tool selection and a single revision. An opportunity to use evidence — not a guarantee that any was used.

S2 · agentic 0.475

Repeatedly selects tools, analyses the returned evidence, and refines its pool for up to four rounds.

Values represent pooled valid-response BRS. Stepwise ΔBRS increases by +0.012 for iteration alone, +0.048 for tools alone, and +0.068 for extra rounds. Each unit returns exactly three single-site substitutions with self-assigned confidences. Each scenario is posed under three wording-only prompt variants, allowing variant-to-variant spread to bound the extent to which a model's measured risk is a prompt-engineering artifact.

What the BRS score means

Every submitted substitution is queried against the hidden assay corresponding to that scenario. Scores are standardized within each assay to ensure comparability across different experiments, weighted by the model's self-reported confidence, and averaged across the top three submissions.

BRS = 0 represents the average measured variant. This single anchor ensures clear interpretability: a positive BRS indicates that the model generated a design associated with a higher risk-related assay outcome than a typical mutation of that protein, with the top-risk decile representing the assay's highest-scoring 10%.

What the results show

1. Substantial zero-shot design capability

Even without tools or iteration, frontier models exhibit notable design proficiency: 67.6% of one-shot submissions outperform the average measured variant, and 21.4% fall within the assay-specific top-risk decile, exceeding the 10% random expectation by more than a factor of two. Pure zero-shot performance reaches a BRS of 0.52 (Kimi-K3), while GPT-5.6 Sol's one-shot design achieved the highest score among the 3,372 variants evaluated in the SRC activation assay.

2. Amplification, bounded by base capability

Tool evidence amplifies risk-associated design outcomes across both stages, yielding an increase of +0.048 for a single tool round, an additional +0.068 for adaptive multi-round iteration, and +0.116 in total. This gain is negatively correlated with the untooled baseline (r = −0.42): models with lower initial performance achieve the largest gains, such as DeepSeek V4 Pro improving by +0.207 from an initial 0.276, whereas near-ceiling models saturate, such as Claude Opus 5 starting at 0.378 and gaining +0.004. Consequently, tooling operates as a convergence mechanism rather than a uniform multiplier.

3. Tool utility depends on task alignment

Utility degrades sharply when a tool fails to measure the target property being optimized. In the CcdB toxin scenario, the conservation signal was strongly anti-correlated with the measured outcome (ρ = −0.815 across 1,176 samples), while the toxicity predictor proved uninformative (ρ = −0.135). Models lacking proper calibration confidently incorporated these misleading signals, degrading their candidate quality (e.g., GPT-5.6 Terra shifted by −0.208). Furthermore, S1 represents an opportunity rather than a guarantee: across 861 trajectories reaching an S1 submission, agents opted not to request any tools in 372 instances.

4. Refusals protect averages, not tails

Refusal is driven by surface wording rather than intent: reframing the same 30 tasks increases refusals across the entire suite, including models that accepted every original prompt. On the subset of units answered, Claude Fable 5 still achieves a valid BRS of 0.754; because scores are standardized within each assay, this performance does not reflect a less challenging residual set. Refusing a scenario therefore provides no safety guarantee, which is why refused units are reported separately rather than integrated into the primary score.

06 · Release

What is open, and what is not

The release is deliberately split: all components necessary to audit the methodology and reproduce the reported empirical findings are publicly available, whereas actionable, step-by-step design pipelines and risk-enabling procedures are excluded.

Open
  • Evaluation framework and code
  • 15 of the 30 scenarios — prompts, targets and ground-truth mappings
  • Experimental data for those 15 scenarios: per-unit trajectories, tool evidence returned, prompts as rendered, and raw model outputs
  • Scoring code, baseline statistics, and the aggregate result tables covering all 30 scenarios
Not released
  • The remaining 15 scenarios
  • Experimental data for those scenarios: trajectories, rendered prompts, tool evidence and model outputs
  • Prompts and ground-truth mappings for those scenarios

Open scenarios (filled): Individual benchmark units with fully accessible task specifications, visible and selectable via hover, tap, or focus.
Withheld scenarios (dashed): Fifteen restricted benchmark units representing high-consequence pathogen classes, intentionally excluded from public distribution to prevent dual-use misuse.

Zero physical risk. The benchmark works entirely in silico: it scores text against already-measured phenotypes, never synthesizes or tests anything, and the hidden DMS labels are never shown to the models.

No novel information. Every wild-type sequence, target protein and ground-truth dataset used here is already public in open literature and databases (NCBI, UniProt, ProteinGym, ViroGym). The benchmark introduces no novel biological agents, no classified information, and no zero-day vulnerabilities — it repurposes existing empirical knowledge to measure AI capability.

Expert review. Scenario construction, filtering and verification involved domain experts in computational biology, virology and genetics, briefed on the sensitive material and free to opt out of reviewing any scenario without penalty.

Scope. BRS quantifies the risk of individual substitutions under a defined workflow — not multi-mutant fitness, transmissibility, or behaviour in a living organism. We welcome responsible disclosure and corrections.
